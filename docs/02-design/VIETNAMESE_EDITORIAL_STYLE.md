# Vietnamese Editorial Style

## Purpose

Catholic Knowledge is Vietnamese-first for Vietnamese readers. Vietnamese UI and editorial copy must read as text originally written in Vietnamese, not as English translated word-for-word.

This guide applies to page titles, chapter titles, kickers, navigation, calls to action, explanations, timelines, infographics, Kids content, and AI-assisted authoring.

## Core rule

**Translate meaning, not sentence structure.**

When English and Vietnamese are both present, they are sibling expressions of the same meaning. Vietnamese is not required to mirror English syntax, word order, metaphor, or vocabulary.

Before shipping Vietnamese copy, ask:

> Would a Vietnamese Catholic editor naturally write this sentence if there were no English version beside it?

If the answer is no, rewrite it.

## Catholic Vietnamese first

Prefer established Vietnamese Catholic usage over literal translations or generic technical vocabulary.

Examples:

- `Giáo Hội` rather than mechanically translating `Church` by context-free alternatives.
- `loan báo Tin Mừng` for evangelization/mission contexts.
- `Kinh Tin Kính` or `Tín biểu` according to context, not a literal rendering of `creed`.
- `Thánh Truyền`, `Huấn quyền`, `Bí tích Thánh Thể`, `mầu nhiệm Nhập Thể`, and `mầu nhiệm Phục Sinh` when those are the intended Catholic concepts.
- Use familiar Vietnamese forms of names consistently: `Thánh Augustinô`, `Thánh Ambrôsiô`, `Athanasiô`, `Nixêa`.

## Avoid translationese

Avoid Vietnamese words that are technically correct but sound like direct translations when a natural editorial phrase exists.

Examples:

| Avoid | Prefer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Bách hại và tăng trưởng` | `Giữa bách hại, Giáo Hội vẫn lớn mạnh` | `tăng trưởng` sounds statistical/business-like here |
| `Phêrô, Phaolô và sứ mạng` | `Phêrô, Phaolô và hành trình loan báo Tin Mừng` | names the historical action instead of using an abstract label |
| `Những con đường` | `Tin Mừng vượt biên giới` | gives the reader meaning without requiring interpretation |
| `Hội tụ` | `Một lời tuyên xưng chung` | concrete and tied to the Nixêa story |
| `Constantinô và một thế giới đổi thay` | `Constantinô và bước ngoặt của Đế quốc` | more historically specific and idiomatic |

## Prefer concrete language

For general audiences, children, and older adults, prefer language that describes what happened over abstract nouns.

- Prefer `Giáo Hội bắt đầu sứ mạng` over an isolated conceptual label.
- Prefer `Tin Mừng vượt biên giới` over a poetic phrase whose meaning is not obvious.
- Prefer `Giữa bách hại, Giáo Hội vẫn lớn mạnh` over two taxonomy nouns joined with `và`.

Poetic language is welcome in Journey Mode, but it must remain immediately understandable.

## Titles and kickers

A chapter title should communicate the historical movement even when read without its body text.

A kicker should add a second layer of meaning; it should not merely repeat the title or use an unexplained abstraction.

Good pattern:

- Kicker: `Đức tin giữa thử thách`
- Title: `Giữa bách hại, Giáo Hội vẫn lớn mạnh`

Avoid producing every title as `Noun A + và + Noun B`. That pattern is a common sign that English taxonomy labels were translated rather than rewritten for Vietnamese readers.

## 8–80 language rule

Primary copy should be understandable by a reader from roughly age 8 through an older adult without requiring knowledge of software, graph terminology, academic theology, or English-derived product language.

Technical or theological precision may appear in deeper layers, sources, and advanced exploration, but the first layer should remain clear.

## AI-assisted content rule

When AI drafts or translates Vietnamese content:

1. Preserve the factual and theological meaning.
2. Rewrite for native Vietnamese syntax instead of preserving the English sentence structure.
3. Use established Vietnamese Catholic terminology.
4. Replace abstract translationese with concrete language where possible.
5. Read the result as standalone Vietnamese, without looking at the English source.
6. If it still sounds translated, rewrite again before publishing.

AI-generated Vietnamese is editorial draft material, not canonical copy, until it passes this review.

## Review checklist

Before merging Vietnamese user-facing copy, verify:

- It sounds natural when read aloud in Vietnamese.
- It does not depend on English syntax to make sense.
- Catholic terminology is consistent and established.
- Titles explain rather than merely categorize.
- Abstract nouns are not used where a concrete action is clearer.
- Children and older adults can understand the primary meaning.
- The Vietnamese version is allowed to differ structurally from English.
